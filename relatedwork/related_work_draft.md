# Related Work

## Multimodal Brain MRI Analysis

Multimodal magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is central to computational brain tumor analysis because T1, T1ce, T2, and FLAIR sequences provide complementary anatomical and pathological cues. Recent BraTS-oriented work has therefore focused on strong 3D segmentation backbones that combine convolutional feature extraction with global context modeling. TransBTS introduced a CNN-Transformer encoder-decoder for multimodal tumor segmentation, while Swin UNETR used a hierarchical Swin Transformer encoder with convolutional decoding for volumetric MRI segmentation [BTS-2021-TRANSBTS; BTS-2022-SWINUNETR]. Challenge systems based on redundancy reduction, confidence ensembling, nnU-Net variants, and multi-model ensembles further demonstrate the maturity of full-modality BraTS segmentation pipelines [BTS-2021-REDUNDANCY; BTS-2022-MULTICNN]. These methods establish effective dense-prediction architectures, but they primarily address voxel-level segmentation under complete or challenge-standard multimodal inputs. By contrast, glioma grading and HGG/LGG classification require patient-level decision representations that remain reliable when one or more MRI sequences are absent.

Brain tumor MRI classification has received comparatively less attention in the missing-modality setting. Prior work on glioma classification has explored explainability-guided training, showing that classification models can be encouraged to focus on tumor-relevant image regions [CLS-2021-XAI]. However, such work does not directly address multimodal absence or structured anatomical reasoning. This leaves a methodological gap between mature multimodal segmentation pipelines and clinically useful patient-level classification models that can operate under variable acquisition protocols.

## Missing-Modality Multimodal Learning

Missing MRI modalities are a practical obstacle for multimodal brain tumor analysis, and recent methods have addressed this problem through synthesis, distillation, alignment, attention, and uncertainty modeling. Synthesis-based approaches attempt to recover absent image information before or during downstream prediction, as in modality synthesis and modality-level attention fusion [MM-2022-MAFNET]. Masked representation learning extends this idea by training models to reconstruct masked modalities or patches and then transferring the learned representation to segmentation [MM-2023-M3AE; MM-2024-MPPA]. These approaches can improve robustness when full imaging is unavailable, but image- or feature-completion pipelines may introduce additional modeling assumptions about unobserved evidence.

Another major line of work treats missing modalities as a representation transfer problem. ACN uses adversarial co-training to reduce gaps between full-modality and missing-modality models [MM-2021-ACN]. DIGEST transfers knowledge from complete-modality teachers to incomplete-modality students through deeply supervised consistency [MM-2022-DIGEST]. More recent work aligns modality representations to reduce complete-incomplete feature gaps, uses graph-guided distillation for weak-modality configurations, or models uncertainty induced by missing evidence [MM-2024-ALIGN; MM-2025-ADAMM; MM-2026-SIUM]. Transformer-based incomplete multimodal learning, represented by mmFormer, further uses modality-specific encoders and inter-modal attention to handle arbitrary available modality subsets [MM-2022-MMFORMER]. Despite their different mechanisms, these methods are predominantly designed for segmentation. Their relational structure is usually implicit in convolutional features, transformer tokens, or teacher-student losses rather than expressed as an anatomical graph over tumor regions.

## Hypergraph Learning for Higher-Order Relations

Hypergraph neural networks provide a natural framework for modeling relations that involve more than two entities. UniGNN generalized graph neural message passing to hypergraphs, while AllSet formulated hypergraph layers as learnable multiset functions over node-hyperedge interactions [HYP-2021-UNIGNN; HYP-2021-ALLSET]. Subsequent work explored diffusion-style hypergraph operators and transformer-based global node-hyperedge interaction [HYP-2022-EDHNN; HYP-2023-HYPERGT]. These methods are relevant to multimodal brain MRI because tumor analysis involves higher-order dependencies among modalities, anatomical regions, and patient-specific imaging patterns. However, most general hypergraph methods are evaluated on abstract node-classification benchmarks rather than on medical imaging tasks with missing acquisition channels.

The closest emerging overlap between hypergraph learning and missing-modality brain MRI is hypergraph-based segmentation under domain-incremental learning. ReHyDIL introduces a cross-patient hypergraph segmentation network for missing-modality brain tumor segmentation and uses domain-incremental learning to accommodate newly available MRI modalities [MM-2025-REHYDIL]. This work indicates that hypergraph modeling is beginning to enter the missing-modality brain tumor literature. Its setting is nevertheless different from missing-modality classification: the hypergraph is used for cross-patient segmentation and incremental domain adaptation, whereas HGG/LGG classification requires a compact patient-level representation that explains how available sequences and tumor regions support the final diagnosis.

## Position of This Work

The above literature suggests a clear but underexplored intersection. Brain tumor segmentation methods provide strong multimodal backbones, missing-modality methods provide robust incomplete-input training strategies, and hypergraph learning provides machinery for higher-order relation modeling. Yet few methods combine these three ideas for patient-level multimodal brain MRI classification. In particular, existing missing-modality methods rarely make anatomical structure an explicit computational object, and existing hypergraph methods rarely address missing MRI sequences in a clinically grounded classification task.

Our work targets this gap with a hypergraph-based missing-modality multimodal MRI classification framework. Instead of synthesizing absent image sequences or relying solely on global attention over feature tokens, the model represents tumor anatomy through ROI nodes and uses mask-aware hyperedges to aggregate only the modalities that are available for each case. It then performs ROI-level reasoning using medical-prior and patient-adaptive hypergraph structure. This two-stage design separates within-ROI modality aggregation from across-ROI tumor-structure reasoning, providing a structured route from incomplete multimodal observations to HGG/LGG prediction. The contribution is therefore not a generic hypergraph architecture, nor another full-modality segmentation backbone, but a missing-modality-aware anatomical hypergraph formulation for multimodal brain MRI classification.
